What's actually going wrong for us

Tagged failure type across all 85 rows, most frequent first.

Ranking problems — 33 rows

Our top 8 sorts the wrong products to the top, or a single visually-close cluster of items (a print family, a fabric pattern) crowds out genuinely on-color garments. This is by far the biggest gap, more than four times any other failure type.

Example — Row 4, Deep red / brick

Hoppn returned deep brick red throughout. We returned a red-and-green patchwork cluster: the same Siena Patchwork Shirt, Iris Patchwork Scarf Tie, and Printed Silk Sarong each shown twice, filling 6 of 8 slots with one print family instead of spreading across genuinely brick-red garments.

Duplicate products in the same top 8 — 113 slots across ~25 rows

The same product (often the exact same listing, sometimes a near-variant) appears twice in one result grid, wasting a slot that could have surfaced a different genuine match.

Off-family or accessory bleed — 9 rows combined

Results include items that are not actually the queried color family, most visibly on gray and metallic queries where silver-toned sneakers and sunglasses got pulled in alongside genuinely gray garments.

Example — Row 83, Gray

Only 2 of 8 results were genuinely gray garments. The rest were white and silver-metallic sneakers plus sunglasses in red, green, navy, and blue accents, pulled in through a silver-metallic association rather than an actual gray hue match. Hoppn returned 7 of 8 genuinely gray-toned items.

Where we're strong

Beige, khaki, and taupe was the one family where we won outright, 4 of 5 rows.

Beige / Khaki / Taupe — 4 of 5 rows won

Clean, on-hue results with no duplicates across most of this family, consistently beating Hoppn's precision on sand, khaki, and taupe tones.

Example — Row 61, Pastel beige / ivory

Our results: 8 of 8 genuine, 8 distinct products, a clean pale warm-white to vanilla to ivory match with zero duplicates.

Neutrals (white–black) — 4 draws, 1 loss

Both apps handle pure black and white queries cleanly. Neither had a hue miss here, and duplication was not a factor.

Bottom line: Hoppn wins on precision and consistency across most saturated hues (red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, indigo), largely because our top-8 ranking surfaces duplicate products and off-target clusters more often than it should. We hold a clear edge on beige, khaki, and taupe tones, and the two apps are effectively tied on blue, purple, teal, pink, and neutral black/white queries.
